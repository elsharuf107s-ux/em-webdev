Start with the cause

Where's the water getting in?

Most masonry trouble starts with water. Here are four common signs, and what each one usually means.

  1. 1

    Crumbling joints

    What it means: Rain soaks into eroded mortar and gets behind the brick face. Sandy joints you can scrape with a key are due.

    Usual fix: Rake out to sound mortar and repoint.

    Tuck pointing
  2. 2

    Flaking brick

    What it means: Water in the brick freezes, expands and pops the face off (spalling).

    Usual fix: Find the water source, replace the damaged brick, repoint around it.

    Brick work
  3. 3

    White powder

    What it means: Efflorescence: salts left behind as water moves through the wall and dries at the surface.

    Usual fix: Trace and stop the water first, then clean. Otherwise it comes back.

    Brick work
  4. 4

    Cracked crown

    What it means: The concrete cap on top of a chimney cracks, and water runs straight down into the brick below.

    Usual fix: Seal or rebuild the concrete crown.

    Concrete

What's the difference between tuckpointing and repointing?

Repointing means raking out failing mortar and replacing it, and that's what most walls need. Tuckpointing originally meant a decorative finish with a thin contrasting line “tucked” into the joint. Most people say “tuckpointing” for both.

What's the white powder on my brick?

That's usually efflorescence: salts carried to the surface by water and left behind when it evaporates. It's normally cosmetic, but it tells you water is moving through the wall.
