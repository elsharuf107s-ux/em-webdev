Start with the cause

Where's the water getting in?

Almost every masonry failure starts with water. Here are the four signs we see most on Calder River valley homes, and what each one usually means.

  1. 1

    Crumbling joints

    What it means: Rain soaks into eroded mortar and gets behind the brick face. Sandy joints you can scrape with a key are due.

    What we do: Rake out to sound mortar and repoint with a mix softer than your brick.

    Tuckpointing & Repointing · from $8/sq ft
  2. 2

    Flaking brick

    What it means: Water in the brick freezes, expands about 9% and pops the face off (spalling). Hard mortar and sealers make it worse.

    What we do: Find the water source, replace the spalled units with matched brick, repoint around them.

    Brick Repair & Replacement · from $750
  3. 3

    White powder

    What it means: Efflorescence: salts left behind as water moves through the wall and dries at the surface. Often a sign of blocked weeps or missing flashing.

    What we do: Trace and stop the water first (weeps, flashing, gutters), then clean. Otherwise it comes back.

    Brick Repair & Replacement · water source first
  4. 4

    Cracked crown

    What it means: The chimney's concrete crown cracks, and water runs straight down into the stack and the ceiling below.

    What we do: Seal or rebuild the crown with an overhang and drip edge, check the flashing, fit a cap.

    Chimney Repair & Rebuilds · from $350

What it covers, and what it doesn't

Covers: defects in our workmanship, such as joints we pointed that crack or fall out, walls we built that lean, and pavers we laid that settle because of our base.

Doesn't cover: damage from water sources we flagged in writing that weren't fixed (gutters, grading, roof leaks), new settlement of the house itself, impact or vehicle damage, de-icing salt misuse, and normal efflorescence on new work (it usually fades in the first year).

How to claim: text a photo. We inspect within 10 business days.

FAQ

Questions homeowners ask about masonry repair

How much does tuckpointing cost?

National cost guides put it around $5-$25 per square foot. Ground-level walls land at the low end, and tall walls that need scaffolding at the high end. Small spot repairs often start around a few hundred dollars. We'll give you a written number after seeing photos or the wall itself.

What's the difference between tuckpointing and repointing?

Repointing means raking out failing mortar and replacing it, and that's what most people need. Tuckpointing originally meant a decorative technique: mortar colored to match the brick, plus a thin contrasting line “tucked” into the joint to make it look finer. Most people (and most search engines) say “tuckpointing” for both, and that's fine. We'll know what you mean.

How do I know if my mortar needs repointing?

Look for joints that are recessed, sandy when you rub them, cracked, or missing. A common rule of thumb is that joints eroded about ¼ inch or more are due. Damp spots inside, or mortar dust at the base of the wall, are other clues.

What's the white powder on my brick?

That's usually efflorescence, salts carried to the surface by water and left behind when it evaporates. It's normally cosmetic and brushes off when dry, but it tells you water is moving through the wall. We find and fix the water source first. Otherwise it comes back.

Why is the face of my brick flaking off?

That's spalling. Water soaks into the brick, freezes, expands and pops the face off. It's often made worse by hard cement mortar or non-breathable sealers that trap moisture. Damaged bricks get replaced, and we fix the moisture problem behind them.

Should my chimney be repaired or rebuilt?

Cracked crowns, worn joints and a few bad bricks can usually be repaired. A stack that leans, has widespread loose brick, or has fire damage often needs rebuilding above the roofline. We show you photos from the roof and explain both options with prices.

Can you match my old mortar and brick?

Usually, yes. We match color, texture and the joint profile, and just as important, the hardness. New mortar should be softer and more breathable than the brick so the joint takes the wear, not your brick. For older homes that often means a lime-rich mix, not straight Portland cement. For bricks, we source matched or salvaged units.

Can masonry work be done in winter?

Exterior mortar work needs air temperatures of about 40°F and rising, so we do most of it spring through fall. In winter we do inspections, estimates, emergency chimney stabilization and indoor fireplace work, and we book spring jobs. Winter is a good time to get on the list.

Do I need a permit for a retaining wall?

In many places, walls 4 feet or taller need a permit and an engineered design, often measured from the bottom of the footing. Some towns require permits for any wall. We check your town's rules and handle the paperwork.

Should I seal my brick?

Usually not as a first step. Many sealers trap moisture and make freeze-thaw damage worse. If a wall needs extra protection after repairs, we use a breathable silane/siloxane water repellent, and only on clean, dry, sound brick.

Pavers or stamped concrete?

Visually similar designs cost about the same up front. Pavers can be lifted and relaid if something settles, while stamped concrete is one slab that can crack and is harder to patch invisibly. We'll show you both.
